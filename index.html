A minimal routine based on Cochrane reviews and dermatology guidance (sources in RESEARCH.md). This is not medical advice: see a doctor for a diagnosis, especially before your first treatment.

1 · Clear it (2–4 weeks)

  • Ketoconazole 2% (Terzolin® 2% Lösung, Apotheke, no prescription): 2×/week. Massage into the scalp, leave 3–5 min, then rinse. Use it as a body wash on the hairline, ears, eyebrows, beard, chest and back too: the same yeast causes the folliculitis.
  • On the other days use a mild shampoo with no red flags, ideally with piroctone olamine, climbazole or selenium disulfide (EU actives; zinc pyrithione is banned in EU cosmetics).
  • Thick, adherent scale: a salicylic acid shampoo or lotion first, so the antifungal reaches the skin.
  • Wash often enough to stop sebum building up. For most people that means every 1–2 days during a flare.

2 · Stop feeding it

  • Scan every leave-on product first: conditioner, styling, oils, serums, beard balm, moisturiser. These matter more than rinse-off products.
  • Conditioner only on the lengths, never the scalp. Rinse face, neck and back last, after the conditioner.
  • No plant oils or butters on scalp or face (coconut, olive, argan, shea, castor).
  • Wash pillowcases, hats and towels often. Shower after sweating and wear breathable fabrics.

3 · Rebuild the barrier (Malassezia-safe)

  • Humectants: glycerin, panthenol, hyaluronic acid, urea ≤5%, niacinamide.
  • Emollients: squalane, MCT oil (caprylic/capric triglyceride), and petrolatum or mineral oil on very dry, non-hairy patches.
  • Fragrance-free products only, lukewarm water, and pat dry. Don't scratch or pick at scale.
  • Patch-test one new product at a time for 1–2 weeks.

4 · Keep it away

  • Ketoconazole every 1–2 weeks for 3–6 months, then as needed. Relapse is normal: SD is chronic, and maintenance treatment keeps it controlled.
  • Rotate between two different antifungal actives if one stops working.

5 · See a doctor (Hautarzt) if

  • There is no improvement after 4 weeks, it is spreading, you have hair loss, oozing or pain, or itchy bumps on your chest or back don't respond.
  • They may prescribe a short course of a mild topical steroid or a calcineurin inhibitor for flares, or an oral antifungal for folliculitis. These are prescription-only, so don't self-medicate.
