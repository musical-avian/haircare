# Hair-care ingredients, Malassezia, seborrheic dermatitis and folliculitis

> Not medical advice. This is a summary of published research and of the community
> "fungal-acne safe" ingredient lists, written to explain the rules in `ingredients.json`.
> Scientific references were retrieved from PubMed (DOIs below).

## 1. Why ingredients matter: Malassezia must eat lipids

- *Malassezia globosa* and *M. restricta* — the species that dominate the scalp and are linked to
  dandruff and seborrheic dermatitis (SD) — **cannot make their own fatty acids**. The *M. globosa*
  genome has no fatty-acid-synthase gene, and instead encodes many secreted lipases and
  phospholipases to harvest lipids from the host (Xu et al. 2007,
  [doi:10.1073/pnas.0706756104](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0706756104)).
- Those lipases split **triglycerides and fatty-acid esters** (sebum, oils, emollient esters) into free
  fatty acids. The yeast consumes the saturated ones and leaves **unsaturated fatty acids, especially
  oleic acid**, on the skin. Oleic acid alone reproduces dandruff-like flaking in susceptible people —
  i.e. the problem is yeast + lipid + individual sensitivity (DeAngelis et al. 2005,
  [doi:10.1111/j.1087-0024.2005.10119.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1087-0024.2005.10119.x)).
- Malassezia lipase-derived fatty acids inducing skin inflammation is the accepted mechanism for SD
  (Harada et al. 2015, [doi:10.1111/1346-8138.12700](https://doi.org/10.1111/1346-8138.12700)).
- Labs grow Malassezia on media whose lipid source is literally **polysorbates (Tween 40/60/80),
  glyceryl esters and oils** (Leeming & Notman 1987,
  [doi:10.1128/jcm.25.10.2017-2019.1987](https://doi.org/10.1128/jcm.25.10.2017-2019.1987);
  Tween-assimilation is a standard identification test, e.g. Puig et al. 2017,
  [doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0179148](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0179148);
  *M. furfur* growth on Tweens 20/40/60/80 and lipid emulsions — Kaneko et al. 2011,
  [doi:10.3109/13693786.2011.584913](https://doi.org/10.3109/13693786.2011.584913)).

Putting a product on the scalp that is rich in exactly these substances adds food and adds the
irritating by-products. That is the basis of the "fungal-acne safe" lists used by the community.

## 2. Ingredient classes

The **chain length** is the useful rule of thumb: fatty acids of roughly **C11–C24** (lauric C12 →
lignoceric C24) and anything that releases them feed Malassezia. **C8–C10** (caprylic, capric) are
too short and are generally considered safe.

### 🔴 Feeds Malassezia — avoid
| Class | Examples on labels |
|---|---|
| Free fatty acids C12–C24 | lauric, myristic, palmitic, stearic, isostearic, oleic, linoleic, behenic, ricinoleic acid; coconut acid |
| Fatty-acid esters | isopropyl myristate / palmitate, ethylhexyl palmitate, glyceryl stearate / oleate, glycol distearate, PEG-100 stearate, cetyl palmitate, ascorbyl/retinyl palmitate, sucrose laurate, polyglyceryl-x oleate |
| Soaps (fatty-acid salts) | sodium cocoate, sodium palmate, potassium oleate, magnesium stearate |
| Polysorbates & sorbitan esters | polysorbate 20 / 40 / 60 / 80, sorbitan oleate / stearate / laurate |
| Triglycerides | most plant oils and butters: coconut, olive, argan, shea, cocoa, avocado, castor, jojoba, sunflower, almond, macadamia, hydrogenated vegetable oil; "triglyceride" (unless caprylic/capric) |
| Wax esters & lanolin | lanolin, cetyl esters, jojoba esters |
| PEG castor oils | PEG-40 hydrogenated castor oil (very common fragrance solubiliser) |

### 🟠 Debated — watch your own reaction
- **Fatty alcohols** (cetyl, stearyl, cetearyl, behenyl alcohol): not esters, most lists say OK, but they
  are long-chain and some people report flares. Almost every conditioner contains one.
- **Ethoxylated fatty alcohols** (ceteareth-20, steareth-2, laureth-4).
- **Ferments** (Galactomyces, Saccharomyces, Lactobacillus) — widely avoided by people with
  Malassezia folliculitis; evidence is anecdotal.
- **Lecithin / phospholipids** — Malassezia secretes phospholipases.
- **Waxes** (beeswax, candelilla, carnauba), **ceramides**, **algae extracts**.
- **Essential oils** (lavender, rosemary, peppermint…) are terpenes, not fatty oils, but irritate inflamed skin.

### 🟢 Generally safe
- Caprylic/capric triglyceride (MCT), caprylic acid, glyceryl caprylate
- Squalane, mineral oil, petrolatum, isododecane, hydrogenated polyisobutene
- Silicones (dimethicone, amodimethicone, cyclopentasiloxane)
- Rinse-off surfactants: sodium lauryl/laureth sulfate, cocamidopropyl betaine, glucosides, isethionates
  (not food, but can be drying)

### 🔵 Helpful actives
Ketoconazole, ciclopirox, zinc pyrithione, selenium sulfide, piroctone olamine, climbazole (antifungals);
salicylic acid, sulfur, coal tar (scale removal). Tea tree oil 5% reduced dandruff 41% vs 11% placebo
(Satchell et al. 2002, [doi:10.1067/mjd.2002.122734](https://doi.org/10.1067/mjd.2002.122734)) but can sensitise.

**Note:** an anti-dandruff shampoo can contain both an antifungal *and* red-flag ingredients (e.g.
Nizoral contains glycol distearate). In a short-contact, rinse-off product with an antifungal that is
far less of a concern than in a leave-in conditioner, oil or styling cream. Leave-on products matter most.

## 3. Seborrheic dermatitis ↔ Malassezia folliculitis

- **Same organism, same food.** Malassezia species are directly responsible for Malassezia (pityrosporum)
  folliculitis (MF) and pityriasis versicolor, and act as an exacerbating factor in SD (Harada et al. 2015,
  above).
- **MF** is yeast overgrowth inside hair follicles: itchy, uniform 2–4 mm papules/pustules on the
  chest, back, shoulders, neck and frequently the **hairline and scalp margin**. It is commonly mistaken
  for acne and doesn't respond to antibacterial acne treatment (Rubenstein & Malerich 2014,
  PMID 24688625; Vlachos et al. 2020, [doi:10.1111/jdv.16253](https://doi.org/10.1111/jdv.16253)).
- **Shared drivers:** sebum-rich areas, heat and humidity, occlusion (heavy oils/butters, hats,
  tight clothing), recent antibiotics (they clear competing bacteria), and immunosuppression
  (Vlachos et al. 2020). Seborrheic areas supply the lipid; occlusive, ester-rich hair products add more
  lipid *and* plug follicles where the yeast is protected.
- **Two-way relationship in practice:** a scalp with active SD has a high yeast load and
  inflamed follicle openings, which seeds MF along the hairline/neck; heavy conditioner and oil residue
  running onto the forehead, neck and back during rinsing is a classic trigger for MF there. Treating
  one with a topical azole (e.g. ketoconazole shampoo used as a 5-minute wash on scalp *and* body)
  usually improves both, and both relapse when lipid feeding resumes.
- **Practical takeaway:** choose leave-on products (conditioner, styling, oils, serums) without red-flag
  ingredients; rinse conditioner off the face/neck/back; keep an antifungal shampoo in rotation.
